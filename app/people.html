Iceland is Europe's most sparsely populated country and one of its fastest-growing: the population rose by a fifth in a decade, and almost all of that growth came from immigration. Two in three people live in the capital region; the rest of the island is, by European standards, empty.

Bigger, older, more international: fertility at a record low.

Empty, on purpose. Almost four-fifths of Iceland is uninhabited — lava fields, glaciers, and highland desert. The population lives on the coast, mostly in the south-west corner, which is why the density figure (3.8 people per km²) understates how urban the country feels.
