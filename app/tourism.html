Two numbers define modern Iceland: 2.26 million visitors a year against 387,758 residents — nearly six guests for every Icelander — and 10,400 km² of glacier that is shrinking every year. This page counts both: who comes, what they spend, and what the land they come to see is doing.

Vatnajökull National Park, about 14% of the country, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019. CO₂ is tonnes of CO₂-equivalent per person, excluding land use. The 8.5 t figure is above the world average of 4.7 t — aluminium smelting and long-distance travel weigh on a small population.

Six guests per Icelander. 2.26 million visitors against 387,758 residents is the highest visitor-to-resident ratio in Europe. Nine in ten visitors arrive through Keflavík, and most never leave the south-west — which is why the pressure is felt in one corner of the island while the rest stays empty.
