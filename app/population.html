Population

Japan's population peaked at 128.06 million in the 2010 census and has fallen every year since 2011. The 2025 census counted 123,049,524 people — down 2.5% from 2020.

Fourteen years of decline

Total population: Statistics Bureau of Japan, Population Estimates; World Bank WDI series. 1 October estimates, million people. Age structure: Statistics Bureau of Japan, Population Estimates, 1 Oct 2024 — children 0–14: 13.83 million (11.2%); working age 15–64: 73.73 million (59.6%); aged 65 and over: 36.24 million (29.3%).

A super-aged society. The 65-and-over share rose from about 21% in 2006 to 29.3% in 2024 and 30.0% in 2025. Japan crossed the “super-aged society” threshold of 21% back in 2007. (Statistics Bureau of Japan; World Bank.)

Migration

Net migration in 2024 was +153,357 people (about +175,000 in 2023). Net migration partly offsets the natural decrease but does not reverse it. Source: UN World Population Prospects 2024 via Worldometer.

Foreign residents registered in January 2026 numbered 4,031,159, out of 123,767,642 total registered residents. Source: Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, Basic Resident Register, Jan 2026.

Looking ahead

The National Institute of Population and Social Security Research projects the population will fall to roughly 87 million by 2070, with about 40% aged 65 or over.
